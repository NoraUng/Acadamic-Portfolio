After moving to the United States, I completed my Bachelor of Business Administration in Management Information Systems, with a minor in Computer Science, at Angelo State University, and I am now pursuing a Master of Science in Computer Science at Texas Tech University. My archival-database work and my projects have shaped my focus on data quality. Specifically, how data becomes unreliable when it is technically valid but inconsistent across systems (for example, a CRM marking a customer "active" while a linked billing system shows them as cancelled).
I'm interested in the thesis pathway at the intersection of data quality, databases, and machine learning, and I'm drawn to research that has a clear, practical payoff for how organizations trust their data.

Research

Research Interests

  • Data quality and validation in relational and tabular data
  • Cross-column semantic consistency detection (numeric, categorical, temporal, and referential errors)
  • LLM-assisted generation of explainable, low-cost validation rules
  • Data mining and machine learning applications in business and healthcare informatics

Detecting Cross-Column Semantic Inconsistencies in Tabular Data Using LLM-Generated Validation Rules

Independent Research Proposal

  • Designing a framework in which an LLM is used once, offline, to generate reusable, human-readable validation rules that encode cross-column relationships, avoiding the cost of running an LLM on every row at inference time.
  • Proposed evaluation against hand-written rules, statistical outlier detection, and direct per-row LLM inference across 2–3 domains (e.g., e-commerce, healthcare, HR), using precision, recall, F1, runtime/API cost, and human-rated interpretability.
